Who we are

A multi-cultural congregation in Te Hui Amorangi ki Te Upoko o te Ika, led by a small ministry team who serve alongside Bishop Waitohiariki.

Leadership

Pihopa Waitohiariki Quayle

Pīhopa o te Hui Amorangi ki te Upoko o te Ika

The first Pīhopa (Bishop) of Te Upoko was the Right Reverend Muru Walters, from his consecration on 7 March 1992 until his retirement in 2018. On 12 September 2019, Bishop Waitohiariki (Wai) Quayle was consecrated as Pīhopa o te Hui Amorangi ki te Upoko o te Ika, and in doing so she became the first Māori woman to become a Bishop.

Bishop Quayle (Ngāti Kahungunu ki Wairarapa and Whakatohea) has a Bachelor of Bicultural Social Work degree from Te Wānanga o Aotearoa, and she worked previously as the Māori Community Health Services Manager at Whaiora Māori Health Services in Masterton.

She was ordained deacon (2013) and priest (2014) by then-Bishop Muru Walters at the Church of Te Hepara Pai in Masterton. From 2015 until her appointment as Bishop, she was the Archdeacon of the Māori Pastorate of Wairarapa.

As Pīhopa of Te Upoko, Bishop Quayle is responsible for guiding Mihinare (Māori Anglicans) in Wellington, Wairarapa, Kāpiti, Horowhenua, Manawatū, Rangitīkei, Whanganui, and Taranaki.

Rev Liz Greville

Co-priest in charge

Reverend Liz Greville is a co-priest in charge at Te Hepara Pai, ordained over 20 years ago by Bishop Tom Brown, serving at the country churches, St Andrew’s in the Paddock, Upper Plain, and St Francis, Bideford, as well as taking services of all sorts at St Matthew’s, including sung Evensong.

My service at Te Hepara Pai under He Amarangi ki te Upoko o te Ika began at the end of 2017. My ministry includes working alongside several catechists in our atrium, using the Catechesis of the Good Shepherd program, the spiritual arm of Montessori. I also serve as a minister, teaching three levels of theological, liturgical, church history, etc. to kaikarakia, deacons and priests.

Outside of my formal job, I am the owner of Coco Chanel, my fabulous, intelligent, gloriously engineered Thermomix. I love making miniatures and one day I’ll get back to lace making and embroidery.

Rev Emily Bartlett

Co-priest

Affiliated with Ngapuhi Iwi from Hokianga, my devotion lies in community ministry and supporting Bishop Waitohiariki. Working alongside my dear friend Rev’d Liz, I serve as a co-Priest with a background rooted in the Ratana and Māori Anglican traditions.

My spiritual journey has taken me from Gisborne to Waikato, eventually leading me here to Te Hepara Pai in 2018. My soul is nourished by the Divine, my grandchildren, and the art of Raranga Harakeke. Whether I am baking on a chilly day or enjoying a quiet moment with coffee, I am constantly reminded of God’s grace protecting our church whanau.

Deacon Richard Jackson

Deacon

I grew up in Hastings and attended the Presbyterian Church. After high school, I joined the Navy and enjoyed a full career of 31 years (most of which was behind a desk, interestingly!). I moved to Masterton in 2000 and commuted to Wellington for 14 years until my retirement. I began attending Te Hāhi Wairua in 2019 and was welcomed into a very supportive congregation. After serving as a Kaitautoko, I entered Deacon training and was ordained in 2024. My personal challenge is to learn Te Reo Māori.

Congregation

People of all ages, cultures, genders and faiths are welcome at Te Hepara Pai.

Our communion services follow the New Zealand Prayer Book and we sing a range of hymns in Maori and English.
